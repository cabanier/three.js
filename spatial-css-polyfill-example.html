Velvet numbers drift past the windows while quiet circuits trade stories about weather, bread, satellites, and old railway maps.

A stack of blue notebooks leans against a brass lamp. Someone has written three impossible passwords on the cover, then crossed out only the vowels.

At the corner table, a calendar insists it is Thursday while the wall clock counts backward in five-minute increments. Neither seems especially worried.

The receipt printer wakes up, prints one line of punctuation, and goes back to sleep beside a cup of paper clips and lemon seeds.

Outside, the sidewalk is busy with umbrellas, bicycle bells, and reflections from shop signs that no one remembers turning on.

The hallway keeps a careful rhythm: paper, glass, footsteps, coffee, paper, glass. Every few minutes the elevator opens for nobody in particular.

Under the desk, a red cable loops around a silver adapter and disappears behind a row of boxes labeled with months that never happened.

By evening, the page has collected enough stray sentences to feel almost organized, though none of them agree on where the story should start.

A courier leaves a blank envelope on the windowsill. The envelope is warm, but the room is not, and everyone decides this is probably normal.
